# Clothed SMV Hypertrophy Recovery Refactor

Date: 2026-05-15

## Evidence Basis

- Volume has a dose-response relationship with hypertrophy, but the marginal return of another set is not free. The routine keeps high-ROI muscles above productive floors while cutting low-ROI or redundant fatigue.
- Failure is not treated as mandatory. Refalo et al. found no clear hypertrophy advantage for momentary failure over non-failure training, so compounds stay mostly 1-2 RIR and isolation failure is limited to clean final sets: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y
- Longer rest is used where performance matters. A 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis found a small benefit to resting over 60 seconds and no clear additional hypertrophy advantage past 90 seconds, so compounds use 150-180 seconds and isolations use 75-90 seconds: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789/full
- Machines, cables, and free weights are all valid hypertrophy tools when the target muscle can be loaded and progressed. Exercise choice is based on stability, availability, target bias, and fatigue cost, not free-weight ideology: https://bmcsportsscimedrehabil.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4
- For lateral delts specifically, a 2025 trained-lifter study found comparable hypertrophy from dumbbell and cable lateral raises when ROM was matched, with lateral deltoid thickness increasing 3.3-4.6 percent over 8 weeks. This supports using DB laterals as a valid Saturday top-up rather than duplicating a second cable slot: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468/full

## Recovery Audit

Muscles trained 3x/week:

- Side delts: Monday, Thursday, Saturday. This is coherent only if per-session dose and failure exposure are capped. The old plan used 16 direct sets with nearly all lateral work at 0-1 RIR. The new plan keeps 14 direct sets but shifts Monday/Thursday to 1-2 RIR with optional clean final-set push.
- Rear delts/posture: Tuesday, Friday, Saturday. This is coherent because rows contribute fractional rear-delt volume and direct rear-delt work is kept at moderate isolation fatigue.
- Biceps/brachialis/forearms: Tuesday, Friday, Saturday. Friday hammer curls replace redundant lat isolation to improve sleeve coverage without adding back fatigue.
- Triceps: Monday, Thursday, Saturday, with pressing overlap. Direct work is kept high enough for sleeve fill, but elbow-stress long-head work is not stacked excessively.

Recovery windows:

- Side delts: Sun-Mon rested, Mon-Thu about 72 hours, Thu-Sat about 48 hours, Sat-Mon about 48 hours. The 48-hour windows are acceptable for small-muscle isolation only because Saturday is a top-up, not another maximal cable-failure session.
- Upper chest: Monday and Thursday, about 72 hours. This is coherent for two stable pressing exposures.
- Lats/mid-back: Tuesday and Friday, about 72 hours. Direct lat isolation was trimmed on Friday to protect pull quality.
- Legs: Wednesday only. Lower-body work is maintenance-biased to avoid systemic spillover into Thursday/Saturday upper-body priority work.

## Redundancy Resolution

The duplicate Saturday cable lateral raise slot is not retained as a second cable slot. It becomes a 2-set DB lateral raise at 2 RIR.

Reason: cables may provide a favorable lengthened resistance profile, but current direct evidence does not show a clear hypertrophy advantage over dumbbells when ROM is matched. The DB slot provides an available, low-setup, shortened-biased top-up and reduces the psychological and local-tissue cost of repeating the same cable pattern to failure.

## Why This Beats Traditional High-Volume PPL For This Goal

Traditional high-volume PPL tends to allocate volume by bodybuilding completeness: more chest angles, more leg work, more back thickness, more pressing, and often similar set counts across muscles. That is not the objective here.

This routine allocates recovery like a scarce resource. It overweights lateral delts, upper chest, lats, rear delts, arms, traps, and neck because those change clothed silhouette fastest. It underweights high-fatigue lower-body and redundant chest/back volume because they cost recovery that could otherwise improve shoulder width, sleeve fill, V-taper, and collar presence.

The corrected system is better because it:

- Keeps high-ROI muscles frequent without forcing all sets to failure.
- Counts indirect volume from presses, rows, and pulldowns.
- Removes redundant Friday/Saturday fatigue where the marginal return was low.
- Uses stable machines/cables/free weights based on SFR, not tradition.
- Preserves legs without allowing leg day to degrade upper-body priority sessions.
- Treats recovery as continuous and tissue-specific rather than assuming every muscle gets the same 72-hour rule.
